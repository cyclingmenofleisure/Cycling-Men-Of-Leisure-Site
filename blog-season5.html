S5E40 · July 27, 2026

Tour De France Finale Breakdown And What It Means Next

The final day of the Tour de France is supposed to be champagne, photos, and a calm roll into Paris. Then the route gets shortened over wildfire security concerns, the temperature climbs, and Tadej Pogacar decides he is not interested in coasting. The Champs-Élysées snapped back into "real race" mode, and it delivered a finale where tactics, pride, and timing mattered just as much as legs.

We walk through how the move formed, why the entire peloton ended up working like a single machine to close it down, and how Mathieu van der Poel turned the last kilometer into a masterclass by hitting the gas at exactly the right moment. Zooming out: Pogacar locks up his fifth Tour de France title at only 27, raising the historical question that won't go away — can he become the first officially recognized six-time winner?

Then there's the podium story that deserves its own spotlight: Isaac del Toro makes history as the first Mexican rider to finish on the Tour de France podium, adding a stage win and the white jersey at 22 while still serving as a loyal teammate. We also get into what made the 2026 Tour feel different — fewer huge crashes than most years, and racing that kept splitting into multiple groups day after day.

To round it out, a quick history hit on why the Champs-Élysées matters, how the Tour's finish moved there in 1975, and a nod to Greg LeMond's unforgettable 1989 finale. What was your favorite moment from Paris?
