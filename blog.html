S5E43 · September 4, 2026

What If Leisure Is The Real Engine Of Labor

A Labor Day episode that starts with a question worth chewing on: what if leisure is the thing that drove labor, and not the other way around? Before the history lesson, though, there's trip prep. Adam and Lauren are eighteen days out from Italy — Rome to Venice by train, a week on a Royal Caribbean sailing around the boot, then Florence, Tuscany, Pisa and three days back in Rome. He fed a 114-page stack of tour itineraries through an LLM and asked it to flag every place the plan could fall apart, and it came back with a dozen pinch points, including the small detail that the cruise doesn't actually end in Rome but 87 kilometers northwest of it. Michael's prediction for the seven-minute train connection at Roma Termini is not optimistic.

The Listener Spotlight is a big one. Bend, Oregon is revealed, and for the first time in a while there are two winners — Melanie Erickson and Armando Rodriguez both nailed it, and Melanie backed it up with a photo of the van parked outside the last Blockbuster on earth. Matt Robinson made a genuinely good case for Grand Rapids, Michigan and came up just short. Elsewhere in community news: Dennis Keeler is in on the January golf outing at PodFest, Tyler Carpenter is back on the bike and riding San Diego to Savannah for the Fuller Center for Housing, and a new subscriber named Terry gets a well-earned thank you.

Then the main event. The first Labor Day was September 5, 1882 — ten thousand workers marching from New York's City Hall to a picnic in Wendel's Elm Park. The guys trace the Haymarket Affair, the Pullman Strike and Grover Cleveland's 1894 signature, why the United States deliberately avoided May 1 (May Day had picked up political baggage abroad), and the long march toward the eight-hour day through the Adamson Act, the Fair Labor Standards Act and Henry Ford's five-day week. The through line is the good part: rank-and-file workers kept trading speeches and picket lines for barbecues and bike races, and the golden age of American velodrome racing landed right in the middle of it. Bicycles helped sell the country on leisure.

Also in the mix: Tadej Pogačar's crash out of the Vuelta a España on stage eight after a dominant opening week, Michigan bicycle crashes down three percent, a two-way cycling track coming to Belle Isle, Team USA's 34-rider squad for the World Championships in Montreal, the return of the Philadelphia Cycling Classic after a decade away, traffic gardens for teaching kids to ride, and Team Amani's push to get East and Central African women to the Tour de France Femmes by 2028. A new set of Spotlight clues closes it out — and Michael admits he's now writing them specifically to stump the AI.

S5E42 · August 21, 2026

A Bass Ate The Golf Ball

Back to the regular rotation after twenty-some days of Tour de France dailies, and the guys ease in with real life: a Michigan Public Transit conference where Adam spoke on a panel about what he wishes he'd known before leading a transit agency, a second year on the board, and the honest admission that the hardest conversations in the job have nothing to do with buses. There's also a conference golf outing, which turns into a clear-eyed look at how business actually gets done — the moment you accept the invitation to play, you've stepped into the vendor's office. Michael checks in from the first weeks of a new job, sitting through training videos and learning a mature organization's way of doing things, plus a proud dad update as marching band and baton season kicks off.

Then the show goes where a cycling podcast has to go. Adam walks through three hard stories: the driver charged after striking the Milligan University cycling team on a training ride in Carter County, Tennessee, killing two riders and injuring seven; nineteen-year-old Welsh pro Finlay Tarling, fatally hit during stage eight of the Volta a Portugal by a van waved onto the course by a bystander who thought the race had passed; and closer to home, longtime friend of the show Tyler Carpenter, who went over the bars on a rails-to-trails ride after the rider ahead of him caught a stick in his front wheel. Everyone in that last one will be okay. The point of telling all three is the same: pay attention out there, and give each other room.

The mood lifts with the Listener Spotlight — Windsor, Ontario, finally revealed, with Jeff and Heather from Michigan taking the win on a voice fan mail. Windsor delivers an unusually rich payload: the automobile capital of Canada, a name borrowed from Windsor Castle, one of the last stops on the Underground Railroad, and the doorway for roughly 75% of the whiskey smuggled into the United States during Prohibition. A fresh set of clues follows, and Michael swears he's handing this one to you. The full clue list lives on the Spotlight page. There's also a milestone worth pausing on: 127,273 downloads, a number fewer than one percent of all podcasts ever reach.

The back half is pure leisure. A LIV Golf pro watches a bass leap out of a water hazard and swallow his ball mid-roll — and still eats the penalty stroke, because the ball had already crossed into the penalty zone. A Missouri club golfer stages a fake hole-in-one for the pot, gets caught on the maintenance shed's security footage, and resigns his membership. Then the bourbon talk turns genuinely useful: Old Fitzgerald Bottled-in-Bond and Penelope's golf series sitting out on open shelves, five Blanton's in a row, Kentucky warehouses holding a record 16.1 million aging barrels, and prices finally softening. If there's a bottle you've always wanted to try, now is the time to go look.

S5E41 · August 7, 2026

A Padded Sports Bra Sparks A Cheating Debate

Somebody texted the show a question neither host expected to hear during Tour de France season: what's up with this cheating women and their bras? That kicks off a surprisingly serious conversation about cycling aerodynamics, the Tour de France Femmes, and allegations that padded sports bras might reduce drag through what researchers call chest fairing. Adam and Michael break down the science in plain language — a Heriot-Watt University study on rounder chest shapes and airflow — talk through what the UCI's rules actually ban, and land on the uncomfortable follow-ups: how many seconds this could really save in a time trial, where equipment optimization ends and cheating begins, and how anyone would even enforce it fairly.

Before any of that, there's a hard left into pure Midwest joy. Adam spent the weekend at a Frankenmuth, Michigan transit conference that included a full-on bus rodeo — obstacle courses, radar guns, wheelchair securement tests, and a "Diminishing Clearance" barrel run that sent barrels flying in every direction. It's funny in the retelling, but it ties back to something real: precision-driving practice cuts down on the low-speed moves that cause the most accidents.

The episode also keeps building out the community side of the show. There's voice fan mail from Alex Bell in Georgia, a Listener Spotlight reveal (the Iron River, Michigan mystery finally cracked open — full clues and story on the Spotlight page), and a walkthrough of new cyclingmenofleisure.com features, including the "Where We Roll" downloads map that tracks listeners by state and continent.

Looking ahead, Adam and Michael start floating a first-ever Cycling Men of Leisure golf outing around Podfest Expo in Orlando — no pressure, no fundraising, just tee times and dinner with anyone who wants to join. Where do you draw the line between marginal gains and cheating?
