Season Five

The Clues & The Reveals

S5E37 → S5E41 · Clues given July 24 · Revealed August 7, 2026

Iron River, Michigan

  • This city was settled in the 1880s
  • It was originally named for a mine
  • At one time it was a booming mining town
  • If you're looking for a home, you might want to look to the trees
  • The government likes this community's trout stream
AnswerIron River, MI

No correct guesses. Alex Bell made a detailed case for Black Diamond, Washington — citing the Black Diamond Mining Company, the town's forested Ten Trails development, and a state fish-passage project on Rock and Ginder Creeks — but the true answer was Iron River, Michigan. Settled between 1880 and 1885 and originally named "Nanamo" (meaning "Iron Mountain") after the mine, it was once a booming mining town; it's known today as the log cabin capital of Michigan; and roughly 17 miles of the Iron River within city limits carry a federal Blue Ribbon Trout Stream designation.

S5E16 → S5E37 · Clues given July 4 · Revealed July 24, 2026

Lewes, Delaware

  • This community is truly a community of firsts
  • The town changed names and hands several times
  • This town has a house named for a piece of ordnance
  • It is the hometown of leaders
  • It is named for a town in Europe
  • Next time you visit, you might want to look to the sky — for financial reward
AnswerLewes, DE

Twenty days of Tour de France dailies kept the guesses away — nobody cracked this one (Adam tried Calais, Maine). The first European settlement in the First State; held by the Delaware people, the Dutch, and the English — once called Swan Valley; the Cannonball House (struck in the War of 1812) now houses the maritime museum; six Delaware governors hail from town; it's named for Lewes, England; and in 2013 a helicopter dropped $10,000 over Lewes Harbor to fulfill a late resident's last wish.

S5E15 → S5E16 · Clues given June 17 · Revealed July 4, 2026

Powell, Tennessee

  • Nicknamed "Mud Town" from the 1930s through the 1960s
  • Travelers might have been confused when they went to fuel up
  • The city has an ESPN connection
  • An early settler included a presidential relation
  • The town's name derives from a transportation stop
AnswerPowell, TN

Bricks of Beaver Creek mud earned the nickname; settler Stockley Donaldson was Andrew Jackson's brother-in-law; and it all started as the Powell Station railroad stop. One guess came in — Abilene, Kansas — not quite.

S5E13 → S5E15 · Clues given May 22 · Revealed June 17, 2026 — the 100th episode

Omaha, Nebraska

  • The town started over lunch
  • The community gave rise to a very unique Boy Scout distinction
  • It found a good use for Russian dressing
  • An invention from this town changed TV viewing habits forever
  • You don't have to be outdoors to enjoy the rain
  • This community was marked by X
AnswerOmaha, NE

🏆 Winners: Paul from Columbia, Missouri (whose fan mail also demanded — and got — the new Listener Spotlight jingle) and Armando. Founded at a July 4, 1854 picnic; Gerald Ford is the only Eagle Scout president; the Reuben was born at the Blackstone Hotel; Swanson invented the TV dinner; the Henry Doorly Zoo has an indoor rainforest; and Malcolm X was born there.

  • The town may have its own dark web

S5E11 → S5E12 · Clues given April 24 · Revealed May 8, 2026

Palestine, Texas

  • Started as a trading post in the mid-1800s
  • Literally named after another town in the United States
  • The town takes balloons seriously

  • Started as a center of trade
  • Alice Cooper may or may not know something about this city
  • German settlers played a large part in the city's development
  • It could be said this was the original — the OG — Blue Ribbon City
  • This city is not San Juan, but one president did overcome adversity there

🏆 Flawless victory — all five guessers got it: Alex Bell (the first-ever voice fan mail guess), Wade Paul, Mark, Matt, and Armando. Wayne's World's "the good land," Pabst Blue Ribbon, and Teddy Roosevelt finishing his speech after being shot.

S5E9 → S5E10 · Clues given March 27 · Revealed April 10, 2026

Eddyville, Kentucky

  • This town was named for a water feature
  • It was settled in the late 1700s
  • It is best known for one of its stone buildings
  • The town knows something about moving
  • It has a strong Colorado connection
  • This city has hosted several counties
AnswerEddyville, KY

Nobody cracked this one — a rare shutout. Eddyville is named for the eddies in the Cumberland River, settled in 1798, and best known for the "Castle on the Cumberland" (the Kentucky State Penitentiary). The whole town relocated in 1959 for the construction of a dam; its Colorado tie is native son John L. Routt, two-time governor of Colorado; and it has served as the county seat of three different counties.

S5E7 → S5E8 · Clues given February 27 · Revealed March 13, 2026

Ashburn, Virginia

  • The city began as an agricultural area — but today it is driven by data
  • The story goes that lightning helped name this town
  • A major airport spurred this town's development
  • The town was in the country for decades, but it was home to a lot of soul

S5E6 → S5E7 · Clues given February 13 · Revealed February 27, 2026

Yuma, Arizona

  • Located at a historical key river crossing
  • Its name was merged with another town's after a major disaster — and later changed again
  • It played an important part in the early military of the area
  • This town knows something about free falling
  • If you visit in the first few days of January, don't order a salad
