S5E43 → S5E44 · Clues given September 4 · Revealed September 18, 2026

Silver Spring, Maryland

  • The town was first settled in the mid-1800s
  • The city is living on the edge
  • The name is in part inspired by a silicate mineral
  • The city was determined that window shopping was overrated
  • Adam appreciates moving people — he would love this community's claim to fame
  • A historical assassin's tool adds to the lure of this community
AnswerSilver Spring, MD

Nobody cracked it — not even Adam, the transit guy, who should have caught the "moving people" clue. (Michael read the sixth clue on air in S5E43, but it never made it onto this page; it's restored above.) Silver Spring was settled in the mid-1800s and lives "on the edge" as an edge city of Washington, D.C.; it's named for a spring flowing with sparkling chips of mica, the silicate mineral; its shopping district was one of the nation's first to put parking out front instead of display windows on the street; the nearby Wheaton Metro station has the longest escalator in the Western Hemisphere — 230 feet, about three minutes to ride; and the National Museum of Health and Medicine holds the bullet that killed Abraham Lincoln. Matt Robinson (a.k.a. Mark) wrote in to say he has no intention of dropping the nickname — it's how fellow listeners find him.

S5E42 → S5E43 · Clues given August 21 · Revealed September 4, 2026

Bend, Oregon

  • This town was not named after a person
  • This city's influence on food is no malarkey
  • It continues to contribute to blockbuster movies
  • Lumber was a driving factor of the economy in the early 1900s
  • This city contributed to the moon landing
AnswerBend, OR

🏆 Two winners — Melanie Erickson and Armando Rodriguez both landed on Bend, and Melanie sent along a photo of the van parked outside the last Blockbuster to prove she'd been there. Matt Robinson (a.k.a. Mark) made a strong run at Grand Rapids, Michigan, but came up short. Bend takes its name from a bend in the Deschutes River where westbound travelers crossed; the "no malarkey" food clue points to celebrity chef Brian Malarkey, a Bend native and a fixture of the competitive cooking circuit; it is home to the last remaining Blockbuster video store on earth — which now makes more money on t-shirts than on rentals; it boomed as a lumber mill town in the early 20th century; and in the 1960s Apollo crews trained on the desolate, moon-like volcanic terrain just outside town.

S5E41 → S5E42 · Clues given August 7 · Revealed August 21, 2026

Windsor, Ontario

  • This town started out as an agricultural community
  • It was incorporated in the mid-1800s
  • The city knows something about cars — it was named for a predominant building
  • The city was a lifeline for what it brought in and what it sent out
  • In the mid-1800s, its neighbors decided the city needed to lighten up
  • Running became a major occupation in the early 20th century
AnswerWindsor, ON

🏆 Jeff and Heather from Michigan cracked it — they worked the clues together the night before and phoned in the guess. Windsor's first settlers arrived in 1749 and the city grew from farmland before incorporating in the mid-1800s; it's the automobile capital of Canada, sitting right across the water from Detroit; it takes its name from Windsor Castle in Berkshire, England; it was one of the last stops on the Underground Railroad and, later, the route for an estimated 75% of all whiskey smuggled into the United States during Prohibition; during the Upper Canada Rebellion some 400 Americans crossed over and attacked the town, burning a steamboat and a couple of homes before the Canadian militia sent them home; and the "running" that became a major occupation was, of course, rum running. Bonus fan mail from Melanie in Seattle, who confessed relief that Alex Bell's Black Diamond, Washington guess missed — she'd have never forgiven herself for missing clues in her own commute.
