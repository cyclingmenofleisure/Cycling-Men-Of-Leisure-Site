Iron River, Michigan

  • This city was settled in the 1880s
  • It was originally named for a mine
  • At one time it was a booming mining town
  • If you're looking for a home, you might want to look to the trees
  • The government likes this community's trout stream
AnswerIron River, MI

No correct guesses. Alex Bell made a detailed case for Black Diamond, Washington — citing the Black Diamond Mining Company, the town's forested Ten Trails development, and a state fish-passage project on Rock and Ginder Creeks — but the true answer was Iron River, Michigan. Settled between 1880 and 1885 and originally named "Nanamo" (meaning "Iron Mountain") after the mine, it was once a booming mining town; it's known today as the log cabin capital of Michigan; and roughly 17 miles of the Iron River within city limits carry a federal Blue Ribbon Trout Stream designation.

S5E16 → S5E37 · Clues given July 4 · Revealed July 24, 2026

Lewes, Delaware

  • This community is truly a community of firsts
  • The town changed names and hands several times
  • This town has a house named for a piece of ordnance
  • It is the hometown of leaders
  • It is named for a town in Europe
  • Next time you visit, you might want to look to the sky — for financial reward
AnswerLewes, DE

Twenty days of Tour de France dailies kept the guesses away — nobody cracked this one (Adam tried Calais, Maine). The first European settlement in the First State; held by the Delaware people, the Dutch, and the English — once called Swan Valley; the Cannonball House (struck in the War of 1812) now houses the maritime museum; six Delaware governors hail from town; it's named for Lewes, England; and in 2013 a helicopter dropped $10,000 over Lewes Harbor to fulfill a late resident's last wish.

Powell, Tennessee

  • Nicknamed "Mud Town" from the 1930s through the 1960s
  • Travelers might have been confused when they went to fuel up
  • The city has an ESPN connection
  • An early settler included a presidential relation
  • The town's name derives from a transportation stop
AnswerPowell, TN

Bricks of Beaver Creek mud earned the nickname; settler Stockley Donaldson was Andrew Jackson's brother-in-law; and it all started as the Powell Station railroad stop. One guess came in — Abilene, Kansas — not quite.
